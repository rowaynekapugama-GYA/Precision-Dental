Part One

Executive
summary.

Most dental practices spend years trying to get where Precision Dental already is: first on Google for "dentist" and "dentist near me" in one of the fastest-growing suburbs in the country, a 5.0 reputation across hundreds of reviews, and a genuine brand as the cosmetic practice in the heart of Brisbane. That position is the whole starting point of this document — and the reason the plan looks nothing like a plan for a practice starting from scratch.

When GYA analysed Precision in 2024, the headline was dominance: top-three for the local terms, number one for the broad ones, a deep organic footprint built over years. That analysis is now eighteen months old, the Valley has changed underneath it, and the strategic question has changed with it. It is no longer "how does Precision get found?" — you are found. It is "where does the next lift in new patients come from, now that organic search is close to maxed out?"

The honest answer is that it comes from three places, none of which is more SEO:

  1. Reaching the patients who never search. A person who wants veneers, implants or Invisalign rarely types anything into Google — they weren't looking for a dentist this morning. That demand doesn't exist in the search auction Precision already wins; it has to be created, on the feed, with a scroll-stopping image and the right offer. That is what Meta does, and §08 is the detailed program.
  2. Converting more of the demand you already earn. Every enquiry that rings and doesn't book, every treatment plan that stalls, every patient who lapses at the nine-month mark and never comes back — that is revenue Precision has already paid to acquire and is leaking. SmileOx is the system that plugs it: instant lead response, structured follow-up, automated recall, and reactivation of a patient database that is years deep. §09.
  3. Putting a growth operator inside the practice. The single biggest lever in an established practice is rarely the marketing — it is what happens after the phone rings and inside the treatment room. David Hills works on that directly: in the practice, coaching the front desk and the team, setting business direction, and reading the leads with you every month. §10, and the reason this proposal exists.
The single most important observation

Organic search rewards the past. Precision's growth from here depends on the two things organic can't do: create demand, and convert it.

Ranking first is a compounding asset built over years, and it should be protected — but it is a demand-capture channel. It catches people already looking. Its ceiling is the number of people searching, and in a category like cosmetic dentistry that number is small relative to the number of people who would say yes to the right offer at the right moment. The practices that break through the organic ceiling all do the same two things next: they run demand-generation on Meta to reach the people who aren't searching, and they build a conversion-and-retention engine so more of that demand becomes booked, treated, retained patients. This proposal is those two moves — plus the person who makes them land operationally.

The engagement is a single monthly growth partnership, offered at three levels — Foundation, Growth, and the Growth Partnership. For Precision we recommend the Growth Partnership, because it is the only tier that includes the two things that will actually move the needle for a practice already winning search: the full SmileOx engine, and David Hills in the practice. §12 sets out all three. Paid media spend on Meta and Google is always paid directly by the practice to the platforms; GYA never marks up media.

The second observation

Being first on Google no longer means being first in the answer.

Through 2025 and into 2026, the overlap between the pages that rank in Google's top ten and the sources that AI assistants actually cite has collapsed — from roughly 70% to under 20% by most measures. In plain terms: a patient asking ChatGPT or Google's AI Overview "who's the best cosmetic dentist in Fortitude Valley?" may get an answer that doesn't mention the practice that ranks first on the classic results page. Precision's years of SEO do not automatically carry into AI answers — that is a separate, winnable job, and one where Precision's 5.0 reputation is a genuine head start. §11.

§ 08
Part Eight

Meta ads
strategy.

This is the growth engine. Precision has never run Meta, which means the single largest untapped source of new cosmetic patients in the practice is currently switched off. Here is exactly how we turn it on.

8.1Why Meta, and why now

The reason to add Meta to a practice that already dominates Google is not "more channels." It's that Meta does something Google structurally cannot: it generates demand rather than capturing it.

Google Ads and organic search both answer a search that has already happened — someone typed "dentist near me," and you compete to be the answer. But the person considering veneers or Invisalign usually isn't searching. They weren't looking for a dentist this morning; they're scrolling Instagram at night. There is no search to win because they haven't searched. Meta reaches that person with a scroll-stopping result and a reason to act — and creates a booking that would never have existed in the search auction at all.

The strategic fit

Precision has maximised demand capture. Meta is demand creation. They're not competing channels — they're the two halves of a whole.

Industry analysis is blunt about this: Meta ads are most effective for practices offering elective, high-value services, precisely because the platform generates demand rather than capturing it — patients considering cosmetic dentistry, implants or Invisalign are rarely searching Google yet. That is the exact description of Precision's growth services and the exact description of Fortitude Valley's young, affluent, image-conscious, Instagram-heavy population. The market and the mechanism line up perfectly, and the channel is sitting unused.

8.2The economics — Australian, 2026

Real numbers from live dental accounts, current for 2026:

$25–75Typical cost per cosmetic / implant lead on Meta
$100–200Cost per booked cosmetic consultation
21%Lower ad costs (CPM) in Australia vs the global average

Set those against value: a single retained cosmetic patient is worth thousands, an implant or full-makeover case far more, and a new general patient retained over years is worth thousands more again. At a cost per booked consultation of $100–200 against case values in the thousands, cosmetic Meta clears comfortably — and Australia's ad costs run materially below global benchmarks, so the maths is better here than the headline US figures suggest. Documented dental cases on this exact model run to 100+ new consultations a month; the ceiling is the practice's chair capacity, not the channel.

The number that makes or breaks Meta

The single biggest determinant of Meta performance in dental is speed and quality of follow-up. A Meta lead that gets an instant, well-structured response books at a dramatically higher rate than one that waits until someone at the front desk has a spare minute — fast AI follow-up alone cuts cost-per-acquisition by 40–60%. This is why Meta and SmileOx (§09) are sold together and why running Meta without a follow-up engine wastes a large share of the spend. The ads create the lead; SmileOx is what turns it into a booking within sixty seconds. Neither works properly without the other.

8.4Targeting

Discipline matters more than cleverness here. The most common way practices waste Meta budget is over-segmentation — stacking ten interest layers on a local audience, which starves the algorithm and drives up costs. We do the opposite:

  • Geography first: a tight radius on the Valley plus the affluent inner-Brisbane catchment — Newstead, New Farm, Teneriffe, Bowen Hills, Bulimba, New Farm, Spring Hill — where the cosmetic-ready professionals live.
  • Demographic layer: age 30–55 for cosmetic and implant messaging, with a household-income qualifier to concentrate on the top income tier; a separate parents-of-children set for family and kids campaigns.
  • Let the algorithm work: broad targeting inside those constraints, optimised toward booked consultations (not raw form fills) via CRM-connected conversion data — which requires the SmileOx integration in §09.

8.5Creative — the part that actually decides it

In 2026, creative is the biggest lever in Meta performance. The plan leans on the assets Precision uniquely has:

  • Real results — presented within AHPRA's advertising rules. Cosmetic transformation content is the highest-converting format in this category, and it must be produced compliantly; GYA writes and frames it that way from the first version, because the alternative is a Dental Board notification.
  • Billy to camera — founder-led video is the single most effective creative type for a cosmetic practice, and Precision has a founder who's comfortable on camera and a genuinely differentiated story to tell.
  • The spa experience — the odour-free rooms, the memory-foam chairs, the blankets, the "forget you're at the dentist" philosophy. This is exactly the anxious-patient reassurance that converts nervous high-value patients.
  • A single, strong offer per ad — the highest-performing dental ads promote one service and one offer, not the whole practice. A "free whitening with new-patient exam"–style hook produces cost-per-lead rates a fraction of a generic "book now."
Compliance is designed in

Every ad, image and offer is built to the Dental Board's advertising guidelines before it runs — testimonials, before-and-after imagery and comparative claims are the three most common triggers for practitioner notifications, and all three are staples of dental Meta advertising. GYA's dental specialisation is precisely what keeps aggressive cosmetic creative on the right side of the line.

8.6Tracking — closed-loop, or it doesn't count

Meta is only worth running if we can prove it produces booked patients, not just leads. Before the first campaign goes live we install the Meta Pixel, the Conversions API (server-side tracking), and full CRM integration through SmileOx, so we track the entire journey: impression → lead → SMS response → consultation booked → case accepted. That closed loop lets us report true return — revenue generated against ad spend, at the campaign level — rather than a vanity lead count, and lets the algorithm optimise toward money instead of form-fills. Media spend on Meta is paid directly by the practice to Meta; GYA never marks up media, and Precision owns the ad account, the pixel data and the audiences permanently.

Meta creates the demand. SmileOx is what converts it — and, just as importantly, what stops Precision leaking the demand it already earns. For an established practice with a database years deep, this is arguably the fastest return in the entire proposal.

9.1What SmileOx is

SmileOx is the dental-specific CRM and automation platform GYA deploys across its practice network. It sits between the marketing and the practice management system and does the one job that quietly determines whether marketing spend converts: it responds to, nurtures, and re-engages every lead and every patient, instantly and consistently, without relying on a busy front desk to remember. 9.2Speed-to-lead — the money is in the first sixty seconds

As §08 stressed, follow-up speed is the biggest single determinant of whether a Meta lead books — fast, automated follow-up cuts cost-per-acquisition by 40–60%. SmileOx responds to every new enquiry — from Meta, the website, or a missed call — with a personalised SMS typically within sixty seconds, then works a structured sequence until the patient books or opts out. A lead that gets an instant, human-feeling reply at 9pm while they're still on the couch books far more often than one that hears nothing until the next afternoon. This alone can be the difference between Meta being profitable and being a waste.

11.1What's actually changed

  • ChatGPT alone reached around 900 million weekly users in early 2026, and AI Overviews now appear on a large and rising share of Google searches — by some measures approaching half.

In plain terms: Precision can be first on Google and simply not appear in the AI answer to the same question.
  • Visitors who arrive from an AI citation convert far better than organic clicks — often several times better — because they arrive already recommended by the assistant.
  • Reputation is a direct input. AI systems weight review quality and review-response rate independently of search ranking, and under-cite businesses rated below 4.0. Precision's 5.0 at scale is a genuine advantage here — one most competitors can't match.
